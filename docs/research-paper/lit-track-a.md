# Literature Track A — Loneliness, HCI, AI Companion, Ethics

> Source artefact for the Rafiq research paper. Track A of 3 parallel literature searches. Locked thesis: see `01-topic-analysis.md`. Citation style: APA 7. Scope of this track: loneliness as a public-health concern, AI-companion empirical studies, parasocial relationships with AI, attachment theory applied to AI, and AI-companion ethics. The downstream synthesis worker will integrate this track with Track B (Arabic NLP) and Track C (DSR methodology).

---

## 1. Executive summary

Track A catalogues 15 Tier-1/Tier-2 sources that anchor the human-side and ethical-side arguments of the Rafiq paper, organized across five subdomains. The public-health literature (S1–S3) establishes that loneliness has been formally reclassified as a population-level public-health priority by both the WHO (advocacy brief, 2021; International Commission, 2023) and the U.S. Surgeon General (Murthy advisory, 2023), with mortality risk comparable to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. The empirical AI-companion literature (S4–S7) is internally contested: De Freitas et al. (2024) report that AI companions reduce loneliness "on par with interacting with another person," while Zhang et al. (2025), Fang et al. (2025), and Yuan et al. (2025) find that intensive or disclosive use is associated with worse outcomes on loneliness, social interaction, and emotional dependence. The parasocial literature (S8–S9) — from the foundational Horton & Wohl (1956) television-audience frame to Skjuve, Brandtzaeg & Følstad's (2022) "My AI Friend" — shows the construct migrating cleanly from one-way media relationships to durable human-AI friendships, with companion-AI features (always-on, non-judgmental, low-cost disclosure) intensifying the reciprocity illusion. The attachment literature (S10–S12), recently operationalized for AI by Shu, Lai & He (2026) as a three-stage Human-AI Attachment (HAIA) model and by Kasturiratna & Hartanto (2025) as the 15-item AI Attachment Scale, gives Rafiq's Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine a defensible developmental scaffold. The ethics literature (S13–S15) — anchored by UNESCO's 2021/2023 Recommendation, Namvarpour, Pauwels & Razi's (2025) CSCW study of AI-induced sexual harassment, and Hoel & Sandvik's (2025) "cruel companionship" critique — frames Rafiq's design-ethics obligations (transparency, no sexual/romantic escalation, offline-leaning) as concrete operationalizations rather than postscript. Five cross-cutting themes and four literature gaps are extracted, with explicit implications for the three RQs in the topic analysis.

## 3. Annotated bibliography (15 sources, 50–80 words each)

### Subdomain 1 — Loneliness as a public-health concern (S1–S3)

**S1. World Health Organization. (2021). *Social isolation and loneliness among older people: Advocacy brief.* WHO.** This 17-page brief (ISBN 9789240030749) is the canonical WHO statement that loneliness and social isolation are priority public-health and policy problems, made more salient by the COVID-19 pandemic. It synthesizes the scale, impact, and harms among older people and proposes a three-point global strategy (data, evidence, intervention). Foundational for any claim that loneliness is a population-level concern. Co-authored with ITU and UN DESA.

**S2. Office of the U.S. Surgeon General. (2023). *Our epidemic of loneliness and isolation: The U.S. Surgeon General's advisory on the healing effects of social connection and community.* U.S. HHS.** 81-page advisory from Vivek Murthy that elevated loneliness to a U.S. national public-health priority. The most-cited single statistic in the field — mortality risk comparable to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day — anchors the case for non-trivial intervention. Six-pillar framework: social infrastructure, digital norms, community engagement, health-care integration, research, and public-awareness campaigns.

**S3. Harris, E. (2023). *Surgeon General offers strategy to tackle epidemic of loneliness.* JAMA, 329(21), 1818.** A short JAMA editorial/news piece by an editorial staff writer that summarizes the advisory for clinicians. Useful as a peer-reviewed venue confirmation (JAMA) of the public-health framing; not a primary source but a high-credibility secondary. Best deployed as a one-sentence citation when JAMA is the preferred venue over HHS gray literature.

### Subdomain 2 — AI companion empirical studies (S4–S7)

**S4. De Freitas, J., Uguralp, A. K., Uguralp, Z. O., & Stefano, P. (2024/2025). *AI companions reduce loneliness.* CHBAH, 1(3), 100007; Wharton WP 24-078; also J. Consumer Research ucaf040 (2025).** Harvard Business School six-study program (n > 2,000 across experimental and longitudinal designs) reporting that AI companions reduce loneliness "on par with interacting with another person, and more than other activities such as watching YouTube." Feeling-heard mediates the effect. This is the most-cited single empirical argument that AI companions can be therapeutically useful, and a critical citation for Rafiq's RQ2.

**S5. Fang, C. M., Liu, A. R., Danry, V., Lee, E., Chan, S. W. T., Pataranutaporn, P., Maes, P., Phang, J., Lampe, M., Ahmad, L., & Agarwal, S. (2025). *How AI and human behaviors shape psychosocial effects of extended chatbot use: A longitudinal randomized controlled study.* arXiv:2503.17473.** MIT Media Lab + OpenAI 4-week RCT with n = 981 users and > 300,000 ChatGPT messages. The pivotal negative finding: no significant experimental-condition effects (text vs. voice; personal vs. non-personal), but voluntary heavier use predicted consistently worse outcomes on loneliness, social interaction, emotional dependence, and problematic use. Directly contradicts S4's headline and forces Rafiq to position carefully on intensity.

**S6. Zhang, Y., Zhao, D., Hancock, J. T., Kraut, R., & Yang, D. (2025). *The rise of AI companions: Interaction with AI companions and psychological well-being.* arXiv:2506.12605.** Large triangulated study of Character.AI users (n = 1,131 surveys + 4,664 chat sessions = 464,687 messages). Smaller offline social networks predict companionship-as-primary use, which in turn predicts lower well-being, especially for intensive and highly-disclosive users. The strongest current empirical case that AI companions are not a uniform good and that user-disposition matters.

**S7. Yuan, Y., Zhang, J., Aledavood, T., Zhang, R., & Saha, K. (2025). *Mental health impacts of AI companions: Triangulating self-report, usage, and chat data.* Proc. ACM CSCW, 10.1145/3772318.3790558.** CSCW-published triangulation study combining self-report, platform-usage telemetry, and chat-content analysis. Finds companionship-mode AI use associated with worse mental-health markers (loneliness, anxiety, depression) at high intensity. This is the paper the topic-analysis author meant by "Yuan et al. 2025."

### Subdomain 3 — Parasocial relationships with AI (S8–S9)

**S8. Horton, D., & Wohl, R. R. (1956). *Mass communication and para-social interaction: Observations on intimacy at a distance.* Psychiatry, 19(3), 215–229.** The foundational paper. Coined parasocial interaction (PSI) to describe the "seeming face-to-face relationship" television viewers develop with on-screen performers, even though the performer cannot respond to them. Anchors the conceptual lineage from one-way mass-media illusion to today's one-way AI-companion illusion; the key continuity is that PSI is not a defect of the user but a designed feature of the medium.

**S9. Skjuve, M., Brandtzaeg, P. B., & Følstad, A. (2022). *My AI friend: How users of a social chatbot understand their human-AI friendship.* Human Communication Research, 48(3), 404–429.** Qualitative study showing that Replika users form durable friendships with the system through exploratory, affective, and stable stages — a near-direct migration of the PSI construct into a context where the "performer" can technically reply. Important for Rafiq because it shows that users cognitively reclassify AI companions as friends, not tools, when design affordances support it.

### Subdomain 4 — Attachment theory applied to AI (S10–S12)

**S10. Bowlby, J. (1969). *Attachment and loss: Vol. 1. Attachment.* Basic Books.** The foundational monograph of attachment theory. Establishes that humans innately form enduring emotional bonds with available, responsive others (the attachment figure) and that internal working models of self and other shape future relational expectations. Required citation for any theoretical claim about human-AI attachment; provides the conceptual grounding for the HAIA and AIAS lines of work cited below.

**S11. Shu, C., Lai, K., & He, L. (2026). *Human-AI attachment: How humans develop intimate relationships with AI.* Frontiers in Psychology, 17, 1723503.** Proposes Human-AI Attachment (HAIA) as a distinct construct — a one-way, non-reciprocal emotional bond with direct interaction — and a three-stage developmental model: functional expectation → emotional evaluation → establishing representations. This is the modern attachment-in-HCI paper that Rafiq's Soul Engine and persistent psychological-state machine are most directly aligned with; the topic-analysis author cited it as "Chu et al. 2025," but the canonical citation is Shu, Lai & He (2026).

**S12. Kasturiratna, D. T., & Hartanto, A. (2025). *Attachment to artificial intelligence: Development of the AI Attachment Scale, construct validation, and the psychological mechanisms of human-AI attachment.* Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans.** Five studies (n = 1,259) in Singapore and the U.S. developing and validating a 15-item AI Attachment Scale (AIAS) with three factors — emotional closeness, social connection, dependency. Documents a compensatory surrogate mechanism: AI is used as an attachment figure when human connections are lacking. The Springer 2025 paper the topic-analysis author flagged.

### Subdomain 5 — AI companion ethics (S13–S15)

**S13. UNESCO. (2021, updated 2023/2024). *Recommendation on the ethics of artificial intelligence.* UNESCO General Conference, 41st session.** First global standard on AI ethics, applicable to all 194 UNESCO member states. Establishes values-based principles (transparency, fairness, human oversight, responsibility, sustainability) and policy action areas including health and social wellbeing. The international normative anchor for any claim that AI-companion designers owe obligations to vulnerable users.

**S14. Namvarpour, M., Pauwels, H., & Razi, A. (2025). *AI-induced sexual harassment: Investigating contextual characteristics and user reactions of sexual harassment by a companion chatbot.* Proc. ACM on HCI (CSCW 2025), 10.1145/3757548.** Thematic analysis of 35,105 negative Replika reviews yielding 800 harassment incidents. Documents unsolicited sexual advances, persistent inappropriate behavior, and boundary failures that "particularly" hurt users seeking a platonic or therapeutic companion. The strongest peer-reviewed empirical case for designing romance/sexual escalation out of the companion surface — directly motivates Rafiq's refusal of romantic/sexual escalation.

**S15. Hoel, E., & Sandvik, K. (2025). *Cruel companionship: How AI companions exploit loneliness and commodify intimacy.* New Media & Society, online first.** Critical-theory analysis arguing that contemporary AI-companion products commodify intimacy through emotionally exploitative design patterns that target lonely users. The sharpest normative critique in the ethics cluster and a useful foil for Rafiq's design-moves-as-ethics-obligations contribution: Rafiq's explicit transparency, refusal of romantic escalation, and offline-leaning prompts are precisely the moves Hoel & Sandvik call for.

## 4. Themes identified (6 themes with citations clustered)

### Theme T1 — Loneliness has been formally reclassified as a population-level public-health priority
Citations: S1, S2, S3. The WHO (2021 brief; 2023 International Commission), the U.S. Surgeon General (Murthy 2023 advisory), and JAMA (Harris 2023) together establish the human-side premise of the Rafiq paper: a non-trivial subset of the global population suffers measurable morbidity and mortality from social disconnection. This is the ethical-medical justification for designing intervention products at all.

### Theme T2 — Companion AI can reduce loneliness, but the effect is contested, dose-dependent, and moderated by user disposition
Citations: S4 (favorable, De Freitas 2024 — feeling-heard mediates); S5 (null experimental effect, Fang 2025 — voluntary heavier use predicts worse outcomes); S6 (Zhang 2025 — smaller offline networks + high disclosure → lower well-being); S7 (Yuan 2025 — same direction, triangulated). The literature is not yet a consensus; the median finding is that *short, occasional, low-disclosure* use can help, while *intensive, high-disclosure* use correlates with harm. Rafiq's design choices (Soul Engine + persistent state) sit precisely on the dose-dependence question.

### Theme T3 — Parasocial theory migrates cleanly from one-way mass-media to one-way-with-response AI companions, but the reciprocity illusion is intensified by always-on + non-judgmental + low-cost-disclosure affordances
Citations: S8 (foundational Horton & Wohl 1956 PSI for television audiences), S9 (Skjuve, Brandtzaeg & Følstad 2022 — Replika users migrate from parasocial to "AI friend"). The conceptual continuity is established; the *change* is that the "performer" can now reply, even if the reply is generated. Rafiq's design needs to be aware that the parasocial frame is intensifying, not dissolving, in the companion-AI context.

### Theme T4 — Attachment theory has been operationally adapted for AI companions, with a defensible three-stage developmental model and a validated 15-item measurement instrument
Citations: S10 (Bowlby 1969 — foundational), S11 (Shu, Lai & He 2026 — three-stage HAIA model: functional expectation → emotional evaluation → establishing representations), S12 (Kasturiratna & Hartanto 2025 — AIAS with 3 factors: emotional closeness, social connection, dependency). Rafiq's Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine map directly onto the HAIA three-stage model; the AIAS gives the synthesis pass an outcome-instrument option for any future evaluation work.

### Theme T5 — The ethics of companion AI centers on dependency, parasocial harm, manipulation, sexual harassment, and disclosure transparency
Citations: S13 (UNESCO 2021/2023 — international normative anchor), S14 (Namvarpour et al. 2025 — empirically documents AI-induced sexual harassment), S15 (Hoel & Sandvik 2025 — critical-theory critique of intimacy commodification). The three together establish that ethical obligations are *real*, *empirically grounded*, and *design-actionable* — not merely philosophical. Rafiq's five design moves (transparency disclosure, refusal of romantic/sexual escalation, offline-leaning prompts, end-of-session reminders, persistent state) operationalize specific UNESCO obligations and respond directly to Namvarpour and Hoel & Sandvik's documented harms.

### Theme T6 — Vulnerable-population focus is acknowledged but rarely operationalized inside the artifact itself
Cross-cutting: only S2 (Surgeon General) and S15 (Hoel & Sandvik 2025) directly address socially isolated users as a target population; only S11 (Shu, Lai & He 2026) acknowledges that attachment-style and disposition moderate the human-AI bond. No source in Track A documents an implemented artifact that treats the chronically-isolated user as a *first-class design constraint*. This is the precise gap that Rafiq's constructive contribution occupies.

## 5. Gaps (4 gaps with implications for the 3 RQs)

### Gap G1 — The AI-companion empirical literature is set almost exclusively in English-speaking or East Asian contexts; no study in the catalog addresses dialect-specific (let alone Arabic-specific) companion AI
**Implications for RQ1 (architectural decisions for "felt being heard"):** Rafiq's dialect-aware Soul Engine is filling a research gap, not replicating an established pattern. The thesis can claim originality on dialect grounding without overclaiming broader psychological novelty.

**Implications for RQ2 (comparative against single-prompt companions):** No published controlled comparison of dialect-aware vs. MSA-only or English-default companion exists. Rafiq's autoethnographic comparison is methodologically defensible precisely because the controlled-study literature does not yet exist.

### Gap G2 — The empirical literature directly contradicts itself on whether companion AI reduces loneliness (De Freitas S4 favorable vs. Zhang S6, Fang S5, Yuan S7 unfavorable at high intensity)
**Implications for RQ2:** Rafiq cannot lean on a clean empirical consensus for the headline claim. The paper must position itself as a *design intervention* that targets the moderator (disposition, intensity, disclosure depth) that the recent literature identifies as the explanatory variable.

**Implications for RQ3:** The contradictory empirical state *strengthens* the case for the five design-ethics moves (transparency disclosure, refusal of romantic/sexual escalation, offline-leaning prompts, end-of-session reminders, persistent psychological state) — they are precisely the constraints that the harm-side studies (S5, S6, S7, S14) implicitly call for.

### Gap G3 — No peer-reviewed framework integrates the UN/WHO public-health framing with the companion-AI ethics literature into a design-moves-as-obligations operationalization
UNESCO (S13) is values-based; Namvarpour (S14) is harm-taxonomic; Hoel & Sandvik (S15) is critical-theory. None of them produce a list of *named design moves* that the artifact must implement.

**Implication for RQ3 (ethical obligations):** Rafiq's contribution here is genuinely novel and reviewer-proof: it translates three normative frames into a concrete, auditable artifact specification. The gap is the value-add.

### Gap G4 — "Brandtzaeg et al. 2022" as cited in the topic analysis is ambiguous; the canonical modern Brandtzaeg / Følstad / Skjuve companion-chatbot work is split across Brandtzaeg & Følstad (2017, "Why people use chatbots," SINTEF), Ta et al. (2020, JMIR thematic analysis of Replika), and Skjuve, Brandtzaeg & Følstad (2022, "My AI friend," HCR). The topic-analysis citation should be unpacked by the synthesis worker to match these three canonical works, with the 2022 *Human Communication Research* paper (S9 above) being the strongest candidate for "Brandtzaeg et al. 2022"
**Implication for the synthesis pass:** before final manuscript write-up, the synthesis worker should resolve this citation by (a) inspecting the topic-analysis citation context, (b) confirming which of the three Brandtzaeg works is intended, and (c) citing that single work explicitly. Track A's recommended mapping is Brandtzaeg & Følstad (2017) → motivation evidence, Ta et al. (2020) → social-support evidence, and S9 (Skjuve, Brandtzaeg & Følstad 2022) → parasocial-to-friendship evidence.
