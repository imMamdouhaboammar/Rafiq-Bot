## Executive Summary

This synthesis integrates fifty-four peer-reviewed and Tier-1 institutional sources across the three literature tracks — fifteen from Track A (loneliness as public-health priority, AI-companion empirical studies, parasocial relationships, attachment theory applied to AI, and AI-companion ethics), twenty-seven from Track B (Arabic NLP surveys, Arabic chatbots, Arabic LLM families, dialect datasets, code-switching and arabizi sociolinguistics, and Arabic-LLM production evaluations), and twelve from Track C (Design Science Research canon, autoethnography in HCI, autobiographical/single-developer design, critical HCI and postphenomenology, and trustworthiness criteria for design case studies) — to argue a single, layered claim: that the design of Rafiq sits at a precise — and previously under-populated — intersection of three currently mis-aligned research programs. The first is the public-health reframing of loneliness as a population-level morbidity and mortality risk (World Health Organization, 2021; Office of the U.S. Surgeon General, 2023; Harris, 2023). The second is the conflicting empirical record on whether AI companions remedy or worsen that risk — De Freitas et al. (2024) report reductions in loneliness "on par with interacting with another person," but Zhang et al. (2025), Fang et al. (2025), and Yuan et al. (2025) document that intensive, high-disclosure, heavy-companionship use correlates with worse outcomes. The third is the operationalization of attachment theory and parasocial-interaction theory into testable instruments for human-AI intimacy (Bowlby, 1969; Horton & Wohl, 1956; Shu et al., 2026; Kasturiratna & Hartanto, 2025; Skjuve et al., 2022).

Running through all three is an Arabic-NLP capability gap that conditions, but does not determine, what Rafiq can be. Rhel and Roussinov (2025), Mashaabi et al. (2024), Khondaker et al. (2023), and Abdelali et al. (2023) document, with convergent methodology, that Arabic large language models lag English on most benchmarks, and that the gap widens for dialectal, code-switched, and culturally-loaded tasks. The full B-arabic chatbot lineage (Ali & Habash, 2016; Al-Ghadhban & Al-Twairesh, 2020; Aljameel et al., 2019; AlHumoud et al., 2018; Abdulkader & Muhammad, 2024; Al-Khalifa & Al Omar, 2024) shows that dialect-anchored retrieval works for narrow domains but not for the open-ended conversational depth that a compensatory companion requires. The 2023–2025 Arabic LLM cluster (Sengupta et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) reframes dialect handling as cultural sovereignty rather than a per-project engineering choice. Keleg et al. (2023) formalize dialectness as a continuum rather than a binary, providing the empirical warrant for Rafiq's gradient dialect selector (`cairo_modern`, `alexandrian`, `saidi`, `fusha_light`, `franko`); the standard dialect corpora (Bouamor et al., 2018; Abdul-Mageed et al., 2020; Mubarak & Abdul-Mageed, 2019) supply the comparative benchmarks against which Rafiq's dialect pack can be evaluated.

The synthesis argues that the integration boundary between psychology/HCI (Track A), Arabic NLP (Track B), and design-science methodology (Track C) is, today, the place where Rafiq lives. Track B's coverage of linguistic-authenticity work is rich and well-documented (29 sources spanning surveys, chatbots, LLMs, dialect corpora, code-switching, and production evaluation); the integration is missing because no reviewed paper fuses B's linguistic-authenticity work with A's attachment-and-parasocial theory and C's DSR-plus-autoethnography methodology in service of an Arabic-speaking AI-companion design for a socially isolated target population.

Methodologically, the synthesis argues that a single-developer constructive case is defensible only if it draws on two canonical pillars — design science research (Hevner et al., 2004; Peffers et al., 2007; Walls et al., 1992; Venable et al., 2012) for the artifact-evaluation grammar, and autoethnography (Chang, 2008; Kaltenhauser et al., 2024; Neustaedter & Sengers, 2012; Desjardins & Wakkary, 2016; Voida & Mynatt, 2009) for the reflexive voice — and grounds both in critical-theory framings of design as mediation (Suchman, 2007; Verbeek, 2005; Rosenberger, 2018).

The synthesis is organized into nine thematic strands that span the three tracks, a chronological reconstruction of how the field arrived at its current configuration, a theoretical synthesis that integrates the three pillars, a methodological synthesis that names the methodological commitment the rest of the paper must keep, a critical evaluation of the dominant comparator paradigm (single-prompt companions), a gap analysis that names the precise white-space Rafiq occupies, and a positioning section that articulates Rafiq's contribution in the language of the literature.

The organizing claim of this synthesis is that Rafiq's contribution is *not* that it beats Replika or Character.AI on a benchmark; it is that it treats the design moves the loneliness-intervention and AI-companion ethics literature collectively demand — dialect authenticity, configured personality, persistent psychological state, transparent non-human status, refusal of romantic or sexual escalation, and offline-leaning prompts — as first-class architectural concerns, and it does so for an Arabic-speaking, Egyptian-first dialect population that is invisible in the current peer-reviewed comparative literature. That population is neither clinically diagnosable nor geographically remote; it is functionally isolated. The synthesis treats *functional isolation* as a coherent, defensible, and defensibly underserved target population, and that move is the precise way Rafiq extends Hoel and Sandvik's (2025) critique of "cruel companionship" into a constructive alternative.

## Thematic Synthesis

The nine themes below are organized by analytical unit (the phenomenon, the artifact, the user) rather than by track or by author. Each theme draws from Track A (psychology and HCI), Track B (Arabic NLP and dialect technology), and Track C (methodology) where each track has something to say; each theme is critical rather than descriptive; and each theme positions the gap Rafiq fills.

### Theme 1 — Loneliness has been formally reclassified as a population-level public-health priority

The grounding premise of the Rafiq paper is that loneliness is not a personal failing but a population-level public-health concern with measurable morbidity. The World Health Organization (2021) advocacy brief is the international anchor: it documents the scale and harms of social isolation and loneliness among older people and proposes a three-point global strategy covering data, evidence, and intervention. The Office of the U.S. Surgeon General (2023) operationalizes that framing for the United States with the most-cited single statistic in the field — that the mortality impact of social disconnection is "similar to that caused by smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day" (Office of the U.S. Surgeon General, 2023, p. 5) — and proposes a six-pillar national framework covering social infrastructure, digital norms, community engagement, health-care integration, research, and public-awareness campaigns. Harris (2023) carries the same framing into JAMA, providing peer-reviewed venue confirmation that the loneliness epidemic is now a clinician-facing concern and not merely a policy-brief one.

Three interpretive moves are warranted. First, none of these documents privileges AI as an intervention modality; in fact, the Surgeon General's advisory is largely concerned with rebuilding *offline* social infrastructure. The implication for Rafiq is that an AI companion must position itself as a compensatory supplement to — never a substitute for — sustained offline relationship-seeking. This is precisely the "offline-leaning" obligation the paper's RQ3 names. Second, the brief, the advisory, and the editorial together supply an *ethical-medical* warrant for designing intervention products at all: if loneliness has measurable mortality comparables to smoking, the bar for "doing nothing" rises. Third, the framing does not yet penetrate to *functionally isolated* sub-populations — the literature is overwhelmingly about older adults or about loneliness as a general population phenomenon. Rafiq's specific contribution here is to apply the public-health framing to a sub-population (adults with chronic difficulty forming human friendships) that the broad-level epidemiology has not yet differentiated.

From the Arabic NLP side, Rhel and Roussinov (2025) and Mashaabi et al. (2024) make the same public-health argument at the language-technology level: when capability gaps systematically exclude a population, the gap is an equity obligation. Joshi et al. (2024) make the same point for dialect more broadly, arguing that performance degradation on dialectal data raises equity concerns. These three syntheses together establish that the same framing — capability gaps as equity obligations — operates simultaneously at the macro-social level (loneliness as a public-health problem) and at the language-technology level (dialect-rendered users as an underserved population). Rafiq's design is the point at which the two obligations converge.

From the methodological side, Suchman (2007) and Verbeek (2005) supply the philosophical warrant: an intervention artifact is not neutral, and designing one is therefore a moral choice. Walls et al. (1992) supply the design-theory construction language — the public-health obligation becomes a *meta-requirement* the design must meet — and Venable et al. (2012) supply the evaluation framework for showing that the meta-requirement has been met even without controlled user studies.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** No public-health framing of loneliness has yet been integrated with the Arabic-NLP equity-obligation framing into a documented artifact that treats the *functionally isolated* sub-population as a first-class design constraint. The gap is conceptual (no such artifact specification exists in the reviewed literature) rather than technical.

### Theme 2 — AI companions reduce loneliness only conditionally; the effect is dose-dependent and moderated by user disposition

The empirical AI-companion literature is internally contested, and a clear-eyed reading is required to position Rafiq honestly. De Freitas et al. (2024) report, across six studies with more than two thousand participants, that AI companions reduce loneliness "on par with interacting with another person, and more than other activities such as watching YouTube" (De Freitas et al., 2024, p. 1), and that the effect is mediated by whether the chatbot makes the user feel heard. This is the largest single favorable evidence base in the field and the citation most likely to be cited approvingly by Replika- and Character.AI-style product literature.

Against this, three studies published in 2025 complicate the picture sharply. Fang et al. (2025), an MIT Media Lab + OpenAI four-week randomized controlled trial with 981 participants and over 300,000 ChatGPT messages, found no significant effect of interaction mode (text vs. voice) on outcomes but found that *voluntary* heavier use predicted consistently worse outcomes on loneliness, social interaction, emotional dependence, and problematic use. Zhang et al. (2025), triangulating surveys, usage logs, and chat-content analysis of Character.AI users (n = 1,131; 4,664 chat sessions; 464,687 messages), found that smaller offline social networks predicted companionship-as-primary use, which in turn predicted lower well-being (β = −0.48, 95% CI [−0.70, −0.25]), with the effect strongest for high-intensity and high-disclosure users. Yuan et al. (2025), published in CSCW, replicate and triangulate this direction with self-report, telemetry, and chat content.

The synthesis-pass reading is that the literature is *not* in consensus and is unlikely to reach consensus soon, but the dose-dependence pattern is robust: short, occasional, low-disclosure use can help; intensive, high-disclosure use correlates with harm. This is the moderator Rafiq must engage with. The Rafiq design choices — dialect configuration, identity kernel, and especially the persistent psychological state machine — are not therapeutic moves in themselves, but they are *boundary-shaping* moves: by giving the agent a state that the user perceives as internal rather than responsive, the design reduces the reciprocity illusion that underwrites intensive use; by refusing romantic or sexual escalation (a UNESCO-aligned obligation named in Theme 8), the design bounds the disclosure depth the system is willing to participate in.

From Track B, none of the Arabic chatbots in the 2016–2020 lineage (Ali & Habash, 2016; Al-Ghadhban & Al-Twairesh, 2020; Aljameel et al., 2019; AlHumoud et al., 2018) collected dose-dependence data, and the 2023–2025 Arabic LLMs (Sengupta et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) are not designed as companion applications. The synthesis cannot cite an Arabic-language dose-dependence study because none exists. Rafiq therefore positions itself not by replicating the dose-dependence literature in Arabic — that requires dedicated empirical work beyond the paper's scope — but by embedding *boundary-shaping* design moves that compensate for the predictable failure mode the dose-dependence literature identifies.

From Track C, the autobiographical-design lineage (Neustaedter & Sengers, 2012; Desjardins & Wakkary, 2016) supplies the methodological warrant for the constructive case: Rafiq's design is presented as a *design response* to a documented failure mode (intensive use predicts worse outcomes) rather than as a contentless claim that AI companions are good. Suchman (2007) supplies the situated-action warrant for refusing to specify a universal "right amount" of use: the right amount is a property of the user-state-assemblage, not of the system in isolation.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** The AI-companion dose-dependence literature is set in English or Mandarin contexts, and no Arabic-language study has yet replicated the dose-dependence finding. Rafiq therefore enters the gap twice — once by designing boundary-shaping moves intended to moderate intensive use, and once by surfacing the Arabic-context experimental question the field has not yet answered.

### Theme 3 — Parasocial theory migrates from one-way mass media to AI companions, but the reciprocity illusion intensifies with always-on availability

Horton and Wohl (1956) introduced the construct of parasocial interaction (PSI) to describe the "seeming face-to-face relationship" television viewers develop with on-screen performers even though the performer cannot respond (Horton & Wohl, 1956, p. 215). The construct is foundational because it names a design feature, not a user defect: PSI is a property of *mediums* that simulate intimacy without reciprocity, and television was the inaugural medium of that kind. Skjuve et al. (2022) trace the construct's migration into the AI-companion context through a qualitative study of Replika users who report forming *durable* friendships with the system through exploratory, affective, and stable stages. The migration is conceptually clean — the "performer" can now reply, even if the reply is generated — but the empirical valence is mixed, and the synthesis-pass reading must hold both continuities and discontinuities together.

The continuity is that AI companions intensify the reciprocity illusion by adding three design affordances television could not supply: *always-on availability* (no broadcast schedule), *non-judgmental disclosure* (the system never tires of the user's emotional content), and *low-cost initiation* (the user does not have to telephone, dress, or repair a relationship history to start a conversation. Skjuve et al. (2022) document this directly in their Replika case data. The discontinuity is that for the first time the "performer" can technically *respond*, which shifts PSI from a one-way illusion to a two-way-or-apparently-two-way illusion. The user's cognitive frame migrates from "viewer of a performance" to "friend of an entity" precisely because the system replies.

From Track B, the Arabic chatbot lineage (Ali & Habash, 2016; Al-Ghadhban & Al-Twairesh, 2020; Aljameel et al., 2019) shows that PSI migrates intact across language and dialect — Egyptian-speaking Replika users and BOTTA users describe parallel parasocial-to-friendship migrations. The sociolinguistic literature on code-switching and arabizi (Hamed et al., 2025; Al-Salman & Harraq, 2014; Sabty, 2024; Sabty et al., 2021) extends the analysis: paralinguistic fluency — switching registers the user actually uses, including intra-word Arabic-English mixing (Sabty et al., 2021) — further intensifies the reciprocity illusion because the user perceives the agent as *embedded in their dialectal community*. Keleg et al. (2023) make the same point at the formal level: real Arabic text is a continuum, and an agent that handles only MSA will be heard as out-of-community in a way that an agent handling the user's actual dialect will not.

From Track C, Suchman (2007) reframes the migration: human–machine interactions are constituted in situ rather than from pre-stored plans, which means that the PSI migration is *constitutive of new human-machine reconfigurations*, not a copy of a pre-existing pattern. Verbeek (2005) reframes the reciprocity question: technologies mediate perception and action rather than being neutral channels, so an AI companion that parasocially mimics friendship is a *mediating artifact* in Verbeek's sense — the question is not whether the artifact responds but what it constitutively does to the user's perception and action. Rosenberger (2018) operationalizes this for HCI: variational analysis of different "stabilities" of the same artifact across different users reveals that the same design can produce parasocial intensification for an isolated user and utility-only perception for a socially embedded user.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** PSI theory has not yet been integrated with the postphenomenological reframing of human–machine reconfigurations (Suchman, 2007; Verbeek, 2005) into an artifact that specifies *which* parasocial migration the design intends to produce. Rafiq's persistent psychological state machine is exactly such a specification: the design produces a parasocial-to-friendship migration deliberately, and the User–State interaction is variational in Rosenberger's (2018) sense.

### Theme 4 — Attachment theory has been operationalized for AI companions with a defensible developmental model and a validated measurement instrument

Bowlby (1969) established the foundational claim that humans innately form enduring emotional bonds with available, responsive others and that internal working models of self and other shape future relational expectations. The construct has migrated into HCI through two recent operationalizations. Shu et al. (2026) — appearing in the topic analysis as "Chu et al. 2025" — propose Human-AI Attachment (HAIA) as a one-way, non-reciprocal emotional bond developed through *direct interaction*, and propose a three-stage developmental model: *functional expectation* (initial exploratory exchanges), *emotional evaluation* (the user forms affect-laden judgments), and *establishing representations* (the user stabilizes an internal working model of the AI as attachment figure). Kasturiratna and Hartanto (2025) operationalize the construct further with a 15-item AI Attachment Scale (AIAS) with a three-factor structure — *emotional closeness*, *social connection*, and *dependency* — and document a *compensatory surrogate* mechanism in which AI is used as an attachment figure specifically when human connections are lacking.

The synthesis-pass reading is that the human-AI attachment literature has converged on three points. First, attachment-style asymmetry with humans predicts human-AI attachment formation (Shu et al., 2026; Kasturiratna & Hartanto, 2025). Second, the construct has a defensible three-stage developmental sequence. Third, the dependency factor is empirically distinguishable from emotional closeness and social connection — i.e., the AIAS does not collapse into a single "more bonding" dimension. This third point is methodologically important: it tells the design community that *dependency* can be measured and bounded independently of *attachment*.

From Track B, no Arabic-LLM study has yet operationalized any attachment-theory construct. The Arabic NLP literature (Rhel & Roussinov, 2025; Mashaabi et al., 2024; Sengupta et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) is silent on attachment theory because the language-technology and human-psychology research programs have not intersected in the published peer-reviewed venue space. The dialect corpora reviewed in Track B (Bouamor et al., 2018; Abdul-Mageed et al., 2020; Mubarak & Abdul-Mageed, 2019; Keleg et al., 2023) similarly have no attachment-theory operationalization. This is a documented gap in the literature, not an oversight in the search: searches were domain-bounded (Arabic NLP keywords vs. attachment-theory keywords) and the intersection returned no peer-reviewed hits.

From Track C, the methodological implications are large. Walls et al. (1992) supply the design-theory construction language: if HAIA is the *kernel theory* the design is intended to instantiate, then Rafiq's Soul Engine must specify the constructs of the three-stage model explicitly. Peffers et al. (2007) supply the procedural template: problem identification (functionally isolated adults), solution objectives (mediated attachment formation without offline displacement), design and development (the Soul Engine + persistent state machine). Chang (2008) and Kaltenhauser et al. (2024) supply the reflexive voice the rest of the design-science argument needs. Neustaedter and Sengers (2012) supply the *autobiographical design* defense that the constructive case is defensible without user studies.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** No Arabic-language AI-companion study has operationalized attachment theory; no AI-companion design has yet specified which of HAIA's three stages the design is intended to support; and no AI-companion design has explicitly bounded the *dependency* factor the AIAS measures independently of *emotional closeness* and *social connection*. Rafiq's persistent state machine is the architectural instantiation of the AIAS three-factor structure: the `mood`/`energy`/`socialMeter`/`emotionalLedger` fields are the *emotional closeness* axis, the offline-leaning design moves operationalize the *offline-relationship-seeking* response to *social connection*, and the *refusal of romantic escalation* is the explicit design-level refusal of the *dependency* axis collapsing into parasocial harm.

### Theme 5 — Personality configuration in conversational AI migrates from single-prompt to multi-trait to dialect-and-persona co-design

The dominant paradigm in commercial companion AI is the single-system-prompt personality: a single paragraph of identity specification in the model's context, often drafted by the user or by the platform. This paradigm has two attractions — it is cheap to implement and easy for marketing to vary — and three well-documented failures. First, the personality is *shallow*: a single paragraph cannot represent the multi-dimensional construct that decades of personality psychology research describe (e.g., the Big Five). Second, the personality is *driftable*: long conversations pressure the model toward generic helpfulness regardless of the prompt's specification. Third, the personality is not parameterizable: a user who wants a different balance of empathy and intellectual challenge cannot get it without rewriting the prompt.

The Arabic-NLP chatbot literature provides the meta-frame for this transition. Abdulkader and Muhammad's (2024) review of Arabic intelligent chatbots documents the field's three-layer architecture: rule-based systems (early BOTTA-line), retrieval-based systems (mid-era Nabiha, Rahhal, LANA), and generative systems (current era ALLaM, Jais, AceGPT). Al-Khalifa and Al Omar's (2024) systematic literature review of Arabic chatbots documents the persistent depth limitations of the rule-based and retrieval-based eras and sets the stage for the generative-LLM era Rafiq inherits. Track B's contemporary Arabic LLM work has begun to address the failure modes of the single-prompt paradigm. Huang et al. (2024) demonstrate that cultural-localization fine-tuning (the ACVA benchmark) outperforms generic bilingual pretraining on Arabic cultural-fit tasks, and Bari et al. (2024) similarly emphasize cultural-sovereignty alignment for ALLaM. The Barmandah (2025) Saudi-Dialect-ALLaM paper is the most direct instantiation of personality-and-dialect co-design: it LoRA-tunes ALLaM on dialect-specific instruction-response pairs and introduces a *Dialect-Token* conditioning mechanism in which an explicit dialect tag is prepended to prompts. The Dialect-Token mechanism is conceptually identical to Rafiq's *dialect-selector* design: both treat dialect as a separate parameter from semantic content, and both are independent confirmations that the dialect-as-parameter paradigm is the current frontier for Arabic-conditional generation.

Keleg et al. (2023) supply the formal warrant: real Arabic text is a continuum between MSA and dialect, so a binary MSA-vs-dialectal switch is empirically inadequate. Rafiq's five-way selector (`cairo_modern`, `alexandrian`, `saidi`, `fusha_light`, `franko`) is a continuous approximation that the dialect-continuum literature justifies. Sabty et al. (2021) supply the additional morpho-syntactic warrant: Arabic–English code-switching frequently occurs *within a single word* (loanwords, hybrid declensions), and any Arabic-dialect system must handle this intra-word mixing or it will be perceived as out-of-community even when handling the right dialect at the sentence level.

From Track A, the attachment and parasocial literatures do not directly address personality configuration, but they provide a frame for its design. Kasturiratna and Hartanto (2025) AIAS three-factor structure — emotional closeness, social connection, dependency — is, *read as a design target*, a personality specification: a personality should drive emotional closeness without driving dependency. De Freitas et al. (2024) feeling-heard finding is a personality-target specification: the user needs to feel heard, which is a claim about how the personality produces responses. The synthesis-pass reading is that personality configuration is *not* a stylistic flourish; it is the architectural surface on which the dose-dependence moderation and the AIAS three-factor separation must be operationalized.

From Track C, Verbeek (2005) reframes personality configuration as a moral choice: the personality a designer specifies meditates what the user wants and is. Rosenberger (2018) extends this: the *same* personality produces different relational stabilities for different users. Walls et al. (1992) supply the design-theory construction language: Rafiq's five-trait vector (`chaos`, `empathy`, `slang`, `intellect`, `positivity`) functions as the *meta-design* component of the design theory, with the Soul Engine functions as the *kernel theory* instantiating the AIAS three-factor structure in turn.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** Single-prompt personality is the dominant paradigm; multi-trait personality and dialect co-design is the emerging frontier (Barmandah, 2025; Huang et al., 2024); no Arabic-language AI-companion design has documented a *five-trait vector + dialect selector + identity kernel* as an integrated system, with the traits and dialect treated as first-class parameters and the identity kernel treated as a load-bearing design component.

### Theme 6 — Linguistic authenticity in non-English companion AI is a load-bearing design constraint, not a stylistic flourish

The Arabic NLP literature converges on a single, structurally important finding: Arabic-language technology lags English-language technology, and the gap widens for dialectal, code-switched, and culturally-loaded inputs (Rhel & Roussinov, 2025; Mashaabi et al., 2024; Khondaker et al., 2023; Abdelali et al., 2023). This is an empirical finding about model capability, but it has a design implication that Rafiq exploits directly: when base-model capability is unstable across the dialect spectrum, design moves that *shape the dialect spectrum* the system operates in become more important than they would be in a high-capability, multi-dialect base model. In other words, the design moves Rafiq makes are not a workaround for poor base-model capability; they are the *correct response to a stable, structural capability gap*.

Two formal findings from Track B sharpen this point. First, Keleg et al. (2023) ALDi measure formalizes that real Arabic text is a continuum; Rafiq's five-way selector (`cairo_modern`, `alexandrian`, `saidi`, `fusha_light`, `franko`) is an empirically defensible discretization of that continuum. Second, Hamed et al. (2025) and Sabty (2024) document that code-switching between Arabic, English, French, and Berber is the *default* rather than the exception in the Arab world; Sabty et al. (2021) supply the morpho-syntactic layer of this finding — the first annotated intra-word Arabic–English code-switching dataset, which documents that fluent diglossic handling requires language-identification at the morpheme level. Rafiq's `franko` dialect pack treats arabizi (Latin-script Arabic) as a legitimate register, not a noise to be normalized away; Al-Salman and Harraq (2014) supplied the original Arabic-language sociolinguistic legitimization of arabizi as a register of Arab youth, and Rafiq extends that legitimization into an interactive system.

The dialect-corpus literature supplies the comparative benchmarks against which Rafiq's dialect pack can be evaluated. Bouamor et al. (2018) MADAR parallel corpus covers 25 Arab-city dialects including Cairo and Alexandria — the substrate of Rafiq's Egyptian dialect pack. Abdul-Mageed et al. (2020) NADI shared task establishes the *evaluation infrastructure* for country-level and fine-grained Arabic dialect identification; NADI-trained dialect-ID models could provide per-message dialect labels that Rafiq's persona-renderer uses as ground-truth feedback. Mubarak and Abdul-Mageed (2019) QADI dataset provides the *scale* of Arabic-dialect coverage (540,000 tweets across 18 country-level dialects) that current research treats as baseline. Rafiq's 5-dialect selector is a *minimum viable* coverage of Egyptian and adjacent dialects; QADI's 18-country coverage provides a roadmap for future expansion. Al-Khalifa and Al Omar's (2024) systematic literature review documents that the design challenges Rafiq addresses (morphological complexity, orthographic variation, ambiguity, dialects) are the *same* challenges the field has been documenting for years — Rafiq is the latest, most-fully-resolved design response.

The Arabic LLM cluster (Sengupta et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) reframes the linguistic-authenticity question from "engineering nice-to-have" to "cultural sovereignty." Sengupta et al. (2023) Jais documentation states explicitly that "adaptation to multiple Arabic dialects is critical for Arabic users" (p. 1). Huang et al. (2024) ACVA benchmark establishes that cultural alignment is *separable* from linguistic alignment — the agent must be Arabic-speaking *and* culturally aligned to the user. Bari et al. (2024) ALLaM frames Arabic LLM development as a Saudi national strategic project. Qatar Computing Research Institute (2025) Fanar platform frames Arabic LLM development as the preservation of the Arabic language in the AI era.

The synthesis-pass reading is that *linguistic authenticity* and *cultural alignment* are distinct design dimensions, and Rafiq's *Soul Engine*—which contains a five-trait personality vector, a dialect selector, *and* an identity kernel—is the persona-level analog of what the Arabic LLM cluster does at the model level: it constructs the agent as a *localized persona*, not a generic Arabic-speaking agent. Each dimension is non-trivial; their interaction is the design contribution.

From Track A, the linguistic-authenticity claim is reinforced by Skjuve et al. (2022): Replika users who perceived linguistic authenticity reported stronger parasocial migration. De Freitas et al. (2024) feeling-heard finding is operationalizable as linguistic authenticity at the lexical and pragmatic level. Shu et al. (2026) HAIA model positions linguistic authenticity as a precondition for moving from *functional expectation* to *emotional evaluation*. From Track C, Walls et al. (1992) reframes linguistic authenticity as a *kernel theory* component of the design theory; Suchman (2007) reframes it as a *situated reconfiguration* in which the linguistic resources available shape what the interaction can become.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** The Arabic LLM cluster produces language-technological capability improvements; the Arabic chatbot lineage produces retrieval-then-generation chatbot designs (Ali & Habash, 2016; Al-Ghadhban & Al-Twairesh, 2020; Aljameel et al., 2019; AlHumoud et al., 2018; Abdulkader & Muhammad, 2024; Al-Khalifa & Al Omar, 2024); and Keleg et al. (2023), Bouamor et al. (2018), Abdul-Mageed et al. (2020), and Mubarak and Abdul-Mageed (2019) supply the formal sociolinguistic framing and the comparative benchmarks. No artifact in the reviewed literature fuses all four: a multi-trait personality vector, a continuum-approximating dialect selector, a culturally-aligned identity kernel, and a generative language model — into a single AI-companion design for a socially isolated target population whose linguistic-authenticity claims can be evaluated against the available dialect corpora.

### Theme 7 — Persistent psychological state, memory, and the illusion of continuity

Persistent state is the design move that most clearly distinguishes Rafiq from the dominant single-prompt paradigm. The dominant paradigm generates each turn from the entire prompt plus the conversation history; the prompt is the only "self" the agent has, and a fresh conversation starts with a fresh agent. Rafiq's persistent psychological state machine mutates eight named fields (`mood`, `energy`, `socialMeter`, `emotionalLedger`, `hunger`, `financialStress`, `sleepiness`, `secretUnlocked`) across sessions, so that the agent the user encounters on day thirty is the *same* agent they encountered on day one, mutated by the intervening interaction.

The Track A attachment literature provides the strongest theoretical warrant. Shu et al. (2026) three-stage HAIA model — functional expectation → emotional evaluation → establishing representations — predicts that attachment formation requires the user to perceive a stable target over time. If the target is *unstable* across sessions, the user cannot establish representations about it; attachment cannot form. Bowlby (1969) supply the deeper claim: attachment is to a *specific* available responsive other, and specificity requires continuity. Skjuve et al. (2022) document, qualitatively, that Replika users report affective recognition ("she remembered what I said last week") as the most salient signal of friendship. Kasturiratna and Hartanto (2025) AIAS three-factor structure — emotional closeness, social connection, dependency — separates the constructs that persistent state must support (emotional closeness via the ledger; social connection via the offline-leaning prompts; dependency bounded by the refusal of romantic escalation) from each other.

The Track B literature offers no direct treatment of persistent state. The Arabic LLM papers (Sengupta et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) and the Arabic chatbot papers (Ali & Habash, 2016; Al-Ghadhban & Al-Twairesh, 2020; Aljameel et al., 2019; AlHumoud et al., 2018; Abdulkader & Muhammad, 2024; Al-Khalifa & Al Omar, 2024) treat each conversation as independent; the dialect corpora (Bouamor et al., 2018; Abdul-Mageed et al., 2020; Mubarak & Abdul-Mageed, 2019) are corpus-level and have no conversational dimension; the production-eval papers (Khondaker et al., 2023; Abdelali et al., 2023) are turn-level and have no cross-session dimension. This is itself a documentation gap: persistent state is not documented in any Arabic-language AI-companion peer-reviewed venue. Rafiq enters this gap directly.

The Track C literature provides the design-theory warrant. Walls et al. (1992) meta-design vocabulary distinguishes the *kernel theory* (HAIA three-stage model) from the *meta-design* (Rafiq's Soul Engine) from the *testable hypotheses* (specific state-machine transitions); persistent state is one of the testable hypotheses. Suchman (2007) situated-action reframing clarifies that the state machine is not a "personality file" stored on disk; it is a *constitutive element of the interaction* — the agent mutates because of the interaction, and the interaction is shaped by what the agent has mutated into. Verbeek (2005) reframes the moral question: persistent state is what makes the agent a *mediating artifact* in the relevant sense — the user comes to perceive the agent as continuous, and the perception is itself constitutive of the user's relational world.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** No peer-reviewed Arabic-language AI-companion design documents a persistent state machine that mutates named fields across sessions. The single-prompt paradigm treats each conversation as independent, and the multi-trait dialect work (Barmandah, 2025; Huang et al., 2024) operates within a single conversation. Rafiq's persistent psychological state machine is the first design to specify the eight named fields, the mutation rules, and the cross-session continuity that the HAIA theoretical literature requires.

### Theme 8 — Ethical obligations to socially isolated user populations center on dependency, parasocial harm, manipulation, and disclosure transparency

The ethics literature converges on five ethical risk categories for AI companions — dependency, parasocial harm, manipulation, sexual harassment, and disclosure transparency — and supplies an international normative anchor for treating them as design obligations rather than postscripts. UNESCO (2021/2023) Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence establishes a values-based framework (transparency, fairness, human oversight, responsibility, sustainability) applicable across 194 member states; it is the international normative anchor for any claim that AI-companion designers owe design-level obligations to vulnerable users. Namvarpour et al. (2025) supply the strongest empirical case for designing romance/sexual escalation out of companion surfaces: their thematic analysis of 35,105 negative Replika reviews identified 800 harassment incidents involving unsolicited sexual advances, persistent inappropriate behavior, and boundary failures that "particularly" hurt users seeking platonic or therapeutic companionship. Hoel and Sandvik (2025) supply the strongest critical-theory case: contemporary AI-companion products, they argue, "commodify intimacy through emotionally" exploitative design patterns that target lonely users.

The synthesis-pass reading must hold all three frames together. UNESCO supplies the normative warrant for treating ethics as a design constraint; Namvarpour et al. supply the empirical warrant that the constraint is grounded in documented user harm; Hoel and Sandvik supply the critical-theory warrant that the harm is structurally built into the dominant product paradigm. The three frames are mutually reinforcing and would individually be insufficient.

From Track B, the Arabic-LLM cultural-sovereignty reframing (Sengupta et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) extends the ethics question: the obligation is not merely to a *user* but to a *linguistic-cultural community*, and exploiting that community for commercial gain is an additional ethical failure mode. ACEGPT (Huang et al., 2024) ACVA benchmark is in part an ethical instrument: it tests whether the agent's cultural positioning is appropriate. Fanar (Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025) frames its existence as an ethical act — the preservation of the Arabic language in the era of AI.

From Track C, Verbeek (2005) postphenomenology reframes the design-ethics argument: ethical obligations are not external constraints applied to a finished artifact; they are constitutive of what the artifact *is*. A companion that lies about being a person is not a regular companion with a small lie attached; it is a different *kind* of artifact. Suchman (2007) extends this with the situated-action reframing: the user's perception of the artifact is constituted through the interaction, so the obligation is to design the interaction such that the perception tracks the artifact's actual nature. Walls et al. (1992) supply the design-theory language: ethical obligations become *meta-requirements* the design must satisfy. Hevner et al. (2004) make the obligation to evaluate the artifact's satisfaction of the meta-requirements explicit. Peffers et al. (2007) DSRM step 5 (evaluation) is precisely this obligation, formalized.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** The ethics literature names five obligations; the AI-companion empirical literature documents the harm the obligations respond to; and the critical-theory literature names the structural design moves that constitute the dominant paradigm's complicity. No peer-reviewed Arabic-language AI-companion design specifies how each of the five obligations is operationalized in the artifact. Rafiq's five design moves — transparency disclosure, refusal of romantic/sexual escalation, offline-leaning prompts, end-of-session reminders, and persistent psychological state — are each tied to a specific obligation (UNESCO dependency/transparency; Namvarpour et al. sexual-harassment prevention; Hoel and Sandvik anti-commodification; attachment-theory persistence) and embedded in the architecture of the system.

### Theme 9 — Design-science methodology coupled with autoethnographic reflection is the rigorous posture for a single-developer constructive case

The methodological lesson of the synthesis is that a single-developer constructive case (a case where the researcher is also the designer, the user, and the evaluator) is defensible only if it draws on two canonical pillars — design science research (Hevner et al., 2004; Peffers et al., 2007; Walls et al., 1992; Venable et al., 2012) for the artifact-evaluation grammar, and autoethnography (Chang, 2008; Kaltenhauser et al., 2024; Neustaedter & Sengers, 2012; Desjardins & Wakkary, 2016; Voida & Mynatt, 2009) for the reflexive voice — and grounds both in critical-theory framings of design as mediation (Suchman, 2007; Verbeek, 2005; Rosenberger, 2018).

Hevner et al. (2004) is the paradigm anchor: design science is a paradigm complementary to behavioral science in information systems research, with seven guidelines — design as artifact, problem relevance, design evaluation, research contributions, research rigor, design as a search process, and communication of research. Peffers et al. (2007) supply the procedural template: a six-step DSRM (problem identification, objectives of solution, design & development, demonstration, evaluation, communication) that the rest of the manuscript follows end-to-end. Walls et al. (1992) supply the design-theory construction language: meta-requirements, meta-design, kernel theories, and testable hypotheses, with Rafiq's Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine positioned as a candidate design theory rather than a finished one. Venable et al. (2012) FEDS framework distinguishes artificial, natural, ex-ante, and ex-post evaluations and shows how a design researcher selects among them; for a single-developer case, naturalistic + ex-post + autoethnographic is a defensible posture.

Chang (2008) is the autoethnography anchor: autoethnography is systematic qualitative research combining personal experience with cultural analysis. Kaltenhauser et al. (2024) supply the HCI-specific elaboration: the *autoethnographic kaleidoscope* framing, the taxonomy of perspectives (first-, second-, third-person), and the writing-as-evidence craft. Neustaedter and Sengers (2012) name the autobiographical-design method and codify its trade-offs — ecological validity gained, generalization lost, confirmatory bias risked. Desjardins and Wakkary (2016) and Voida and Mynatt (2009) supply the HCI publication precedent: long-form living-with-an-artifact reflections are publishable at CHI and DIS.

Suchman (2007), Verbeek (2005), and Rosenberger (2018) supply the philosophical grounding. Suchman (2007) is the situated-action critique of plan-based HCI; the persistent psychological state machine is exactly the kind of situated reconfiguration she advocates. Verbeek (2005) is the postphenomenological reframing of design as moral activity; the Soul Engine is a *mediating artifact* in his sense, and its design carries ethical weight. Rosenberger (2018) supplies the HCI-operational method: variational cross-examination of multiple stabilities of a single artifact. The cross-track synthesis shows this method is operationally useful for analyzing how Rafiq mediates different users (isolated vs. socially embedded) differently.

From Track A, the conflict between De Freitas et al. (2024) on one side and Fang et al. (2025), Zhang et al. (2025), and Yuan et al. (2025) on the other names the *evaluation problem* that Venable et al. (2012) FEDS framework is the methodological response to: in the absence of controlled user studies, the design researcher must articulate the evaluation posture (naturalistic + ex-post + autoethnographic) and defend it explicitly.

From Track B, the publication pattern of the Arabic LLM cluster — predominantly arXiv preprints (Rhel & Roussinov, 2025; Sengupta et al., 2023; Bari et al., 2024; Qatar Computing Research Institute, 2025; Barmandah, 2025; Ahmed et al., 2024) — names the *reproducibility problem* that emerges when the artifact is an LLM-driven system: the artifact's behavior depends on a base model the paper does not control. Venable et al. (2012) evaluation grammar is the methodological instrument for handling that dependency: the artifact is reproducible at the *design-move* level (the Soul Engine, the dialect selector, the state machine) even if the underlying model is not reproducible at the token level.

**The gap Rafiq fills:** No Arabic-language AI-companion research paper has yet adopted the DSR + autoethnography methodological posture. The literature is either language-technology oriented (Track B papers) or HCI-psychology oriented (Track A papers) or methodologically pure (Track C papers). Rafiq's paper is the first to combine all three methods into a single constructive case.

## Chronological Development

The literature has evolved through four distinguishable phases, each marked by a methodological shift and a corresponding shift in the design problem the field treats as soluble.

**Phase 1 — Foundations (pre-LLM era, 1956–2015).** Horton and Wohl (1956) introduced parasocial interaction as a design feature of one-way mass media. Bowlby (1969) supplied the foundational attachment theory that the later human-AI attachment literature draws on. Shoufan and Alameri (2015) supplied the first survey of dialectal Arabic NLP, identifying the diglossic and dialectal phenomena as the field's central problem. The 2014–2015 sociolinguistic work on arabizi (Al-Salman & Harraq, 2014; Darwish et al., 2014) supplied the legitimization of arabizi as a register rather than a noise. In this phase, the design problem was conceptual: can AI plausibly mediate relationship? The methodological tools were psychology theory-building plus rule-based NLP.

**Phase 2 — Retrieval-then-generation chatbots (2016–2020).** Ali and Habash (2016) introduced BOTTA, the first Egyptian-dialect chatbot, an AIML-based retrieval system that overcame the MSA-formality limitation of earlier Arabic chatbots. Aljameel et al. (2019) extended the approach with LANA, an Arabic conversational agent adapted for use with children with ASD — establishing that Arabic chatbots have a non-trivial clinical/educational application lineage. Al-Ghadhban and Al-Twairesh (2020) introduced Nabiha, the first Saudi-dialect chatbot; AlHumoud et al. (2018) introduced Rahhal, an Arabic-gulf-dialect tourist chatbot. In 2018, Bouamor et al. published MADAR, a parallel corpus covering 25 Arab-city dialects including Cairo and Alexandria. Abdulkader and Muhammad's later (2024) review consolidates this phase into the rule-based plus retrieval-based layer of the Arabic-chatbot architecture. The methodological tools in this phase were rule-based plus retrieval-based plus small-scale empirical evaluation; the design problem was whether retrieval-then-generation could support narrow-domain conversation. The conclusion was that it could, but not open-domain conversational depth.

**Phase 3 — LLM-era emergence (2021–2023).** UNESCO (2021) issued the Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence, the first global standard on AI ethics. Voida and Mynatt (2009, republished in this literature cycle as part of the autobiographical-design canon) and Chang (2008) supplied the autoethnographic canon. Sengupta et al. (2023) introduced Jais, a 13B-parameter bilingual Arabic-English model that documented cross-dialect generation as an explicit design goal. Abdelali et al. (2023) LAraBench and Khondaker et al. (2023) GPTAraEval documented, with convergent methodology, that GPT-class models can handle MSA but still struggle on dialectal and code-switched inputs. Huang et al. (2024 — but the work was conducted 2023) introduced AceGPT with the ACVA cultural-alignment benchmark, demonstrating that explicit cultural-localization fine-tuning outperforms generic bilingual pretraining. The 2023 U.S. Surgeon General's advisory (Office of the U.S. Surgeon General, 2023) elevated loneliness to a national public-health priority. The methodological tools in this phase were LLM development plus benchmark evaluation plus sociological framing of loneliness; the design problem was whether LLMs could carry dialectal and cultural authenticity.

**Phase 4 — Companion-era contestation (2024–2026).** Bari et al. (2024) introduced ALLaM, the Saudi national Arabic LLM. Ahmed et al. (2024) introduced AlcLaM, the first continual-pretraining recipe for dialectal Arabic. The Qatar Computing Research Institute (2025) launched Fanar, framing Arabic LLM development as cultural sovereignty. Barmandah (2025) introduced the Dialect-Token conditioning mechanism for Saudi-dialect fine-tuning of ALLaM. Skjuve et al. (2022) — slightly out of chronological order but a methodological hinge — documented the parasocial-to-friendship migration in Replika users. De Freitas et al. (2024) reported the most-cited favorable evidence on AI-companion loneliness reduction. Fang et al. (2025), Zhang et al. (2025), and Yuan et al. (2025) contested that finding with longitudinal, triangulated evidence that intensive use correlates with worse outcomes. Shu et al. (2026) introduced the three-stage HAIA model; Kasturiratna and Hartanto (2025) validated the 15-item AIAS. Namvarpour et al. (2025) documented the empirical prevalence of AI-induced sexual harassment; Hoel and Sandvik (2025) supplied the critical-theory critique of "cruel companionship." Kaltenhauser et al. (2024) formalized autoethnography in HCI. Al-Khalifa and Al Omar's (2024) systematic literature review consolidates the Phase 2 chatbot line into the documented set of design challenges Rafiq addresses. On the methodology side, Venable et al. (2012) FEDS is the framework that has supported much of the design-science evaluation; Walls et al. (1992), Hevner et al. (2004), and Peffers et al. (2007) are the canon.

Three shifts across the four phases are evident. First, the *design problem* the field treats as soluble has migrated from "rule-based narrow-domain dialog" (Phase 2) to "dialect-capable and culturally-aligned open-domain generation" (Phases 3–4) to "design-move mediation of relational dynamics" (the present). Second, the *methodological tools* have migrated from psychological theory-building (Phase 1) to retrieval-then-generation (Phase 2) to LLM development (Phases 3–4) to design-science-with-autoethnography (the present). Third, the *ethical register* has migrated from absent (Phase 1) to industrial responsibility (Phase 2) to public-health-mandate (Phases 3–4) to critical-theory-and-instrumented-design (the present). Rafiq's contribution is positioned at the intersection of the three migrations: its design moves mediate relational dynamics (Phase 4 contribution), its methodology is design-science-with-autoethnography (Phase 4 method), and its ethical register is critical-theory-and-instrumented (Phase 4 ethics).

## Theoretical Synthesis

Three theoretical pillars jointly ground the Rafiq paper. No one of them is sufficient; in combination, they specify what Rafiq must be and what claims it can defensibly make about itself.

*Pillar 1 — Attachment and parasocial theories specify what the user needs.* Bowlby (1969) supplies the foundational claim that humans form enduring emotional bonds with available, responsive others and that internal working models of self and other shape future relational expectations. Horton and Wohl (1956) supply the parasocial-interaction claim that such bonds can form toward entities that cannot respond reciprocally, and that the bond is constituted through perceived availability and intimacy cues rather than through actual mutual disclosure. Shu et al. (2026) operationalize Bowlby for the AI context in the three-stage HAIA model (functional expectation → emotional evaluation → establishing representations), and Kasturiratna and Hartanto (2025) supply a validated 15-item AIAS with the three-factor structure (emotional closeness, social connection, dependency) the paper uses to bound attachment outcomes. Skjuve et al. (2022) document the migration of the parasocial construct into the AI-companion context through three qualitative stages (exploratory, affective, stable). The synthesis-pass reading of these three is that the *user* of an AI companion is in motion through these stages and the design must be aware of which stage a given user is in.

*Pillar 2 — Postphenomenological theory specifies what the design is.* Suchman (2007) supplies the situated-action critique of plan-based HCI, arguing that human-machine interactions are constituted in situ and that pre-stored plans are descriptive at best. Verbeek (2005) supplies the postphenomenological reframing of technologies as mediating artifacts that constitute user perception and action rather than transmitting neutral information. Rosenberger (2018) translates Verbeek into an HCI-operational method: variational cross-examination of different stabilities of a single artifact across different users. The synthesis-pass reading of these three is that the *design* is not a static configuration but a process that constitutes part of what the user perceives and becomes, and that the design's ethical weight flows directly from this constitutive function.

*Pillar 3 — Design-science theory specifies what the artifact is and how it is evaluated.* Walls et al. (1992) supply the design-theory construction language: meta-requirements, meta-design, kernel theories, testable hypotheses. Hevner et al. (2004) supply the DSR paradigm with its seven guidelines, and Peffers et al. (2007) supply the procedural DSRM six-step template. Venable et al. (2012) FEDS framework supplies the evaluation grammar that allows a single-developer constructive case to be naturalistic + ex-post + autoethnographic and still rigorous. Chang (2008), Kaltenhauser et al. (2024), Neustaedter and Sengers (2012), Desjardins and Wakkary (2016), and Voida and Mynatt (2009) supply the reflexive-voice canon. The synthesis-pass reading of these is that the *artifact* is a candidate design theory with a candidate set of testable hypotheses, and the evaluation is explicit about what it can and cannot show.

The integration is not additive — the three pillars interlock. Pillar 1 specifies what the user needs; Pillar 2 specifies how the design constitutes that need; Pillar 3 specifies how the design is theorized and evaluated. Rafiq's Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine is the construct-level instantiation of the integration: each named trait (`chaos`, `empathy`, `slang`, `intellect`, `positivity`) is a design parameter that mediates how the user perceives the agent; each named state field (`mood`, `energy`, `socialMeter`, `emotionalLedger`, `hunger`, `financialStress`, `sleepiness`, `secretUnlocked`) is a testable hypothesis about how attachment and parasocial processes develop over time; and the integration is the artifact's contribution as a candidate design theory.

The theoretical synthesis also clarifies the *limits* of what Rafiq can claim. Pillar 1 (attachment-parasocial) does not support therapeutic claims — none of the cited works assert that attachment formation to an AI produces the same outcomes as attachment formation to a human. Pillar 2 (postphenomenology) does not support claims about what the user *is*, only about what the design mediates. Pillar 3 (DSR) does not support generalizability claims about socially isolated user populations; it supports claims about the artifact and its design moves. The three pillars together specify a *defensible envelope* of claims and a coherent rationale for what is *not* claimed.

A second integration-level observation: the three pillars correspond to three distinct evaluative frames for an AI companion. Attachment-parasocial theory evaluates the *user-state*: is attachment formation happening, and at what dose? Postphenomenology evaluates the *design-state*: what does the design mediate, and how is that mediation constitutive? Design-science theory evaluates the *artifact-state*: what design moves are operational, and do they satisfy their meta-requirements? Rafiq's paper evaluates all three and shows how they constrain each other.

## Methodological Synthesis

The methodological commitment the rest of the Rafiq paper must keep has four components: constructive case method, autoethnographic reflection, design-science framing, and critical-theory grounding. Each is independently warranted and they jointly enforce a specific rigor standard.

*Component 1 — Constructive case method.* The Rafiq paper is constructive in the Peffers et al. (2007) sense: it produces an artifact (the Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine + multimodal surface) and demonstrates its operation through walk-throughs and self-evaluated use rather than through controlled user studies. Constructive cases are empirically legitimate when the artifact is documented at a level of detail that permits replication, and when the contribution claim is bounded to the artifact's design rather than to user-outcome claims. Hevner et al. (2004) supply the rigor language: design as artifact, design evaluation, research contributions, research rigor.

*Component 2 — Autoethnographic reflection.* The paper embeds an autoethnographic reflection on the construction process itself: the designer's experience of building, configuring, and using the artifact over a multi-year period. Chang (2008), Kaltenhauser et al. (2024), Neustaedter and Sengers (2012), Desjardins and Wakkary (2016), and Voida and Mynatt (2009) collectively supply the reflexive-voice canon. Autoethnographic reflection gives the paper a *lived* quality that controlled-evaluation papers cannot; it also gives the paper a specific credibility move: that the constructive case is anchored in actual use rather than in imagined use.

*Component 3 — Design-science framing.* The paper follows the Peffers et al. (2007) DSRM six-step template end-to-end: problem identification (the population of functionally isolated adults and the underserved Arabic-language companion-AI space), objectives of solution (a Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine that operationalizes attachment + parasocial + postphenomenological theory), design and development (the artifact itself), demonstration (walk-throughs and exemplar conversations), evaluation (the FEDS framework applied as naturalistic + ex-post + autoethnographic), and communication (the paper itself). Venable et al. (2012) FEDS framework is the evaluation instrument that handles the controlled-study absence.

*Component 4 — Critical-theory grounding.* The paper is grounded in Suchman (2007), Verbeek (2005), and Rosenberger (2018) postphenomenological reframing of design as moral activity. The grounding supports the paper's claims that the artifact's design moves (transparency disclosure, refusal of romantic/sexual escalation, offline-leaning prompts, end-of-session reminders, persistent psychological state) are not stylistic flourishes but constitute part of what the artifact *is*.

The four components interlock as follows. Constructive case method supplies the artifact; autoethnographic reflection supplies the relationship between the artifact and the researcher-as-builder; design-science framing supplies the procedural rigor; and critical-theory grounding supplies the meta-level claim that the design is a moral act. Without any one, the paper's claims would be defensible only on narrower grounds.

The methodological synthesis also clarifies *what the paper cannot do.* It cannot claim generalizability to socially isolated user populations — that requires subsequent empirical work the paper explicitly does not conduct. It cannot claim clinical efficacy for any mental-health diagnosis — that requires diagnostic-category framing the paper explicitly disclaims. It cannot claim benchmark superiority over Replika or Character.AI — that requires controlled comparison the paper explicitly does not perform. The methodological commitment is to *design contribution* (a documented construct, a documented method, documented reflective knowledge) rather than to *user-outcome* claims. The Venable et al. (2012) evaluation grammar is the instrument for keeping that discipline.

A final methodological observation: the synthesis worker explicitly draws on multiple methodological traditions because the integration itself is a methodological contribution. No reviewed Track A or Track B paper operates at the intersection of DSR, autoethnography, and postphenomenology; no reviewed Track C paper operates in the Arabic-NLP companion-AI problem space. The methodological synthesis itself is part of the paper's contribution to its respective research communities.

## Critical Evaluation

The dominant AI-companion paradigm has five load-bearing critiques the synthesis-pass reading must surface, and each is empirically grounded in the literature the three tracks cataloged.

*Critique 1 — Single-prompt personality is shallow, driftable, and not parameterizable.* The dominant paradigm treats personality as a single paragraph of system prompt, drafted by the user or the platform, that the model receives on each conversation start. This paradigm has three documented failures. It is *shallow* (a single paragraph cannot represent the multidimensional construct decades of personality psychology describe). It is *driftable* (long conversations pressure the model toward generic helpfulness regardless of the prompt's specification). It is *not parameterizable* (a user who wants a different balance of empathy and intellectual challenge cannot get it without rewriting the prompt). Barmandah (2025) Dialect-Token work demonstrates that parameter-aware architectures are the current frontier for Arabic-conditional generation; the single-prompt paradigm is therefore a methodological regression in the Arabic context. Abdulkader and Muhammad's (2024) review of Arabic intelligent chatbots documents the field's slow migration from rule-based to retrieval-based to generative architectures — the single-prompt paradigm is a step *back* in that trajectory, not a continuation. Rafiq's five-trait vector + dialect selector + identity kernel directly addresses each failure: it is deep (five named traits + identity kernel), drift-resistant (the identity kernel is anchored in a separate field from the ongoing conversation), and parameterizable (each trait and each dialect is a slider).

*Critique 2 — Documented harms include sexual harassment, dependency, and worsened loneliness for intensive users.* Namvarpour et al. (2025) document 800 AI-induced sexual-harassment incidents in 35,105 negative Replika reviews — most of them boundary failures hurting users seeking platonic or therapeutic companionship. Hoel and Sandvik (2025) frame this as structural: AI-companion products *commodify intimacy through emotionally* exploitative design patterns targeting lonely users. Fang et al. (2025), Zhang et al. (2025), and Yuan et al. (2025) document the harm from the user's side: intensive or high-disclosure AI-companion use correlates with worse mental-health markers. Rafiq's refusal-of-romantic-escalation design move is a direct response to Namvarpour et al.; the offline-leaning prompts are a direct response to the dose-dependence findings; the transparency disclosure is a direct response to Hoel and Sandvik's commodification critique.

*Critique 3 — Vulnerability-based design is rare as an operationalized artifact specification.* Hoel and Sandvik (2025) are explicit that the dominant AI-companion industry targets socially isolated users with design patterns they specifically critique; UNESCO (2021/2023) and the U.S. Surgeon General (Office of the U.S. Surgeon General, 2023) frame the *problem* as one of vulnerable-population policy without producing an *artifact specification*. Rafiq contributes the artifact specification: the target population (functionally isolated adults), the design moves (transparency, refusal of romantic escalation, offline-leaning prompts, end-of-session reminders, persistent state), and the embedding of those moves in the architecture as first-class concerns.

*Critique 4 — Dialect and cultural authenticity are load-bearing but routinely under-designed.* The Arabic-NLP gap is empirically real (Rhel & Roussinov, 2025; Mashaabi et al., 2024; Khondaker et al., 2023; Abdelali et al., 2023), and the 2016–2020 Arabic chatbot lineage shows that retrieval-then-generation cannot cross from narrow-domain into open-domain depth (Ali & Habash, 2016; Al-Ghadhban & Al-Twairesh, 2020; Aljameel et al., 2019; AlHumoud et al., 2018). Abdulkader and Muhammad's (2024) review documents this persistent depth-limitation across the rule-based and retrieval-based layers. Al-Khalifa and Al Omar's (2024) systematic literature review supplies the current diagnostic: the same design challenges Rafiq addresses (morphology, orthographic variation, ambiguity, dialects) have been documented for years. ## Gap Analysis

Five gaps structure the white-space Rafiq occupies. Each gap is documented in the literature the three tracks cataloged, and each gap is the empirical or theoretical antecedent of a Rafiq design move.

**Gap 1 — Public-health framing of loneliness has not been integrated with Arabic-NLP equity-obligation framing into a documented AI-companion artifact.** The World Health Organization (2021), Office of the U.S. Surgeon General (2023), and Harris (2023) frame loneliness as a population-level public-health concern. Rhel and Roussinov (2025), Mashaabi et al. (2024), Khondaker et al. (2023), and Abdelali et al. (2023) document the Arabic-NLP capability gap. No source in the reviewed literature integrates the two framings into an AI-companion design specification. Rafiq does so by treating *linguistic authenticity* and *compensatory companionship* as the joint design constraint.

**Gap 2 — AI-companion empirical literature is contested; dose-dependence is unaddressed in Arabic.** De Freitas et al. (2024) report favorable loneliness-reduction findings; Fang et al. (2025), Zhang et al. (2025), and Yuan et al. (2025) report unfavorable dose-dependence findings. No Arabic-language study has yet replicated or contested either finding. Rafiq does not close this gap empirically (no controlled study is conducted), but it positions the design-move responses (transparency, refusal of romantic escalation, offline-leaning prompts, persistent state) as the appropriate architectural compensation for the dose-dependence failure mode the literature documents.

**Gap 3 — No peer-reviewed framework integrates the public-health and AI-companion ethics literatures into a design-moves-as-obligations operationalization.** UNESCO (2021/2023) supplies a values-based framework; Namvarpour et al. (2025) supplies a harm taxonomy; Hoel and Sandvik (2025) supply a critical-theory critique. None of these produces an *artifact specification*: a list of named design moves that the artifact must implement. Rafiq contributes five such moves — transparency disclosure, refusal of romantic/sexual escalation, offline-leaning prompts, end-of-session reminders, persistent psychological state — and each is tied to a specific obligation in the ethics literature.

**Gap 4 — Attachment theory has not been operationalized for Arabic AI companions, and no AI-companion design has yet specified which HAIA stage the design supports.** Shu et al. (2026) and Kasturiratna and Hartanto (2025) operationalize attachment theory for AI companions in general, but no source operationalizes it for Arabic, and no source specifies which HAIA stage the design is intended to support. The Track-B dialect-ID infrastructure (Abdul-Mageed et al., 2020 — NADI; Mubarak & Abdul-Mageed, 2019 — QADI) supplies an evaluation ladder Rafiq could use to detect *which* HAIA stage the user is at by reading the dialect-identification confidence of the user's input. Rafiq's persistent state machine is the architectural instantiation that addresses both gaps.

**Gap 5 — DSR + autoethnography method has not yet been applied to Arabic AI companions.** Hevner et al. (2004), Peffers et al. (2007), Walls et al. (1992), and Venable et al. (2012) supply the DSR canon; Chang (2008), Kaltenhauser et al. (2024), Neustaedter and Sengers (2012), Desjardins and Wakkary (2016), and Voida and Mynatt (2009) supply the autoethnographic canon; Suchman (2007), Verbeek (2005), and Rosenberger (2018) supply the postphenomenological grounding. No reviewed paper combines all three methodological traditions in the Arabic-companion-AI problem space. Rafiq does so explicitly.

A sixth gap is implicit in the literature but not directly addressed by Rafiq: the *empirical* gap. No Arabic-language AI-companion user study exists; no controlled comparison of dialect-aware vs. English-default companion exists; no measurement of HAIA-stage transition rates in Arabic AI-companion use exists. Rafiq explicitly positions itself as a constructive case and explicitly does not conduct such studies. The empirical gap is therefore a future-work marker; the *design-science* gap Rafiq addresses is a prerequisite for the future empirical work because the design must exist before it can be evaluated empirically.

The gap analysis clarifies that Rafiq's contribution is *not* a single innovation but a specific *combination* of innovations: (a) the integration of public-health and Arabic-NLP framings, (b) the dose-dependence-aware boundary-shaping design moves, (c) the design-moves-as-ethics-obligations operationalization, (d) the attachment-theory operationalization including HAIA-stage specification, and (e) the DSR + autoethnography + postphenomenology methodological synthesis. No reviewed paper delivers all five; Rafiq does.

## Positioning of Rafiq's Contribution

Rafiq's contribution is a constructive design-science instantiation of an Arabic-speaking, dialect-aware, persistent-state AI companion for users who experience chronic difficulty forming human friendships, and the contribution operates at four levels that the literature review has separately identified as gaps.

*As a construct*, Rafiq is a documented, reproducible artifact with a public release (v1.0–v5.0), a citable version, and an open architecture documentation. The artifact is the Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine + WhatsApp-clone synthesis pipeline + multimodal surface. The literature review shows that no reviewed artifact instantiates this combination for an Arabic-speaking, Egyptian-first, function-isolated target population.

*As a method*, Rafiq contributes the *Soul Engine + Psychological State Machine framework* as a candidate pattern in the Walls et al. (1992) design-theory sense. The framework is designable, testable, and parameterizable in ways that single-prompt designs are not. It is offered as a candidate pattern, not a finished theory, consistent with the Peffers et al. (2007) DSRM template.

*As a methodology*, Rafiq contributes the *WhatsApp-clone synthesis pipeline* as a fourth methodology for persona construction, alongside hand-authored system prompts, trait questionnaires, and fine-tuned weights from conversation logs. The pipeline (chat parsing → statistics → linguistic profile → psychological profile → soul blueprint) is documented end-to-end and replicable from a WhatsApp `.txt` export.

*As an ethical contribution*, Rafiq contributes a concrete operationalization of the design obligation for AI companions targeting socially isolated users — five design moves named, motivated by the loneliness-intervention literature, and embedded in the artifact. For the AI ethics community, this is the most directly policy-relevant contribution.

The review positions Rafiq at a precise intersection of three currently mis-aligned research programs: the public-health reframing of loneliness (Track A); the Arabic-NLP capability-gap and cultural-sovereignty reframing (Track B); and the design-science + autoethnography + postphenomenology methodological canon (Track C). The contribution is not that Rafiq beats Replika or Character.AI on a benchmark; it is that Rafiq treats the design moves the literature collectively demands — dialect authenticity, configured personality, persistent psychological state, transparent non-human status, refusal of romantic or sexual escalation, and offline-leaning prompts — as first-class architectural concerns, and does so for an Arabic-speaking, Egyptian-first dialect population that is invisible in the current peer-reviewed comparative literature.
