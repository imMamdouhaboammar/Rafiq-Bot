## 2. Source list (12 sources)

### Cluster A — Design Science Research canon

#### 1. Hevner, A. R., March, S. T., Park, J., & Ram, S. (2004). Design science in information systems research.
> **APA 7 entry:** Hevner, A. R., March, S. T., Park, J., & Ram, S. (2004). Design science in information systems research. *MIS Quarterly*, *28*(1), 75–105.

- **Type:** Foundational journal article.
- **Tier:** 1 (canonical DSR anchor — >15,000 citations).
- **Note (50–80 w):** Defines design science as a paradigm complementary to behavioral science in IS research. Articulates seven guidelines for conducting and evaluating DSR (relevance, rigor, design as artifact, problem relevance, design evaluation, research contributions, communication). For Rafiq, this is the load-bearing methodological anchor — the Soul Engine + Psychological-State Machine is the artifact, and the seven guidelines supply the evaluation grammar.

#### 2. Peffers, K., Tuunanen, T., Rothenberger, M. A., & Chatterjee, S. (2007). A design science research methodology for information systems research.
> **APA 7 entry:** Peffers, K., Tuunanen, T., Rothenberger, M. A., & Chatterjee, S. (2007). A design science research methodology for information systems research. *Journal of Management Information Systems*, *24*(3), 45–77. https://doi.org/10.2753/MIS0742-1222240302

- **Type:** Methodology journal article.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Proposes the six-step Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM): problem identification, objectives of solution, design & development, demonstration, evaluation, communication. Supplies the procedural template the Rafiq paper follows end-to-end. **Discrepancy:** the task brief lists "Peffers et al. 2008" — the canonical publication year is 2007 (Vol. 24, No. 3, Winter 2007–08 issue but the printed cover year is 2007).

#### 3. Walls, J. G., Widmeyer, G. R., & El Sawy, O. A. (1992). Building an information system design theory for vigilant EIS.
> **APA 7 entry:** Walls, J. G., Widmeyer, G. R., & El Sawy, O. A. (1992). Building an information system design theory for vigilant EIS. *Information Systems Research*, *3*(1), 36–59.

- **Type:** Foundational theory article.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Introduces the meta-requirements / meta-design / kernel theories / testable hypotheses construction for IS design theory. Gives the Rafiq paper its theoretical-formulation scaffolding: the Soul-Engine + Psychological-State-Machine framework is offered as a candidate design theory rather than a finished one — directly aligned with Walls et al.'s criteria.

### Cluster B — Autoethnography in HCI

#### 4. Chang, H. (2008). *Autoethnography as method*.
> **APA 7 entry:** Chang, H. (2008). *Autoethnography as method*. Left Coast Press.

- **Type:** Methodological monograph.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Foundational text that legitimizes autoethnography as systematic qualitative research combining personal experience with cultural analysis. Distinguishes evocative autoethnography (Ellis tradition) from analytic autoethnography (Anderson tradition); Rafiq's reflective voice sits closer to the analytic pole (analyst-as-builder commenting on their own artifact with cultural-critical distance). No DOI (print monograph).

#### 5. Kaltenhauser, A., Stefanidi, E., Schönbeck, T. J., Averin, K., Dingler, T., Memar, A. H., & Draxler, F. (2024). Playing with perspectives and unveiling the autoethnographic kaleidoscope in HCI: A literature review of autoethnographies.
> **APA 7 entry:** Kaltenhauser, A., Stefanidi, E., Schönbeck, T. J., Averin, K., Dingler, T., Memar, A. H., & Draxler, F. (2024). Playing with perspectives and unveiling the autoethnographic kaleidoscope in HCI: A literature review of autoethnographies. In *Proceedings of the 2024 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (Article 819, pp. 1–20). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642355

- **Type:** Conference review article.
- **Tier:** 1 (CHI 2024).
- **Note (50–80 w):** First systematic literature review of autoethnography in HCI; documents the method's spread, taxonomizes the perspectives autoethnographers occupy (first-person, second-person, third-person), and codifies the writing-as-evidence craft. For Rafiq, this is the direct precedent for using autoethnographic reflection inside a constructive design paper — the kaleidoscope framing also lets the synthesis worker frame the analyst-as-builder voice.

### Cluster C — Autobiographical / single-developer design

#### 6. Neustaedter, C., & Sengers, P. (2012). Autobiographical design in HCI research: Designing and learning through use-it-yourself.
> **APA 7 entry:** Neustaedter, C., & Sengers, P. (2012). Autobiographical design in HCI research: Designing and learning through use-it-yourself. In *Proceedings of the 2012 ACM Annual Conference on Designing Interactive Systems (DIS '12)* (pp. 514–523). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/2317956.2318034

- **Type:** Conference methodology paper.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Names the method "autobiographical design" and codifies its trade-offs: the researcher-as-user gains ecological validity for everyday-life context but loses generalization and risks confirmatory bias. Supplies the methodological justification for Rafiq's single-developer constructive case being defensible without user studies. Companion *Interactions* essay (Nov/Dec 2012) makes the case for general HCI audiences.

#### 7. Desjardins, A., & Wakkary, R. (2016). Living in a prototype: A reconfigured space.
> **APA 7 entry:** Desjardins, A., & Wakkary, R. (2016). Living in a prototype: A reconfigured space. In *Proceedings of the 2016 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems* (pp. 5274–5285). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858261

- **Type:** Conference design case study.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Documents two researchers inhabiting a custom-built smart-home prototype for a year — concrete instantiation of autobiographical design at the artifact scale relevant to Rafiq. Shows how long-form living-with-an-artifact surfaces tensions (privacy, repair, dependency) that short-term evaluations miss; mirrors Rafiq's 2023–2026 development arc.

#### 8. Voida, S., & Mynatt, E. D. (2009). It feels better than filing: Everyday work experiences in an activity-based computing system.
> **APA 7 entry:** Voida, S., & Mynatt, E. D. (2009). It feels better than filing: Everyday work experiences in an activity-based computing system. In *Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI '09)* (pp. 259–268). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/1518701.1518744

- **Type:** Conference first-person HCI study.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Earlier first-person HCI precedent: researchers lived with their own activity-based computing system and reported the experiential findings in the first person. Useful for Rafiq because it demonstrates that HCI publication venues (CHI) accept researcher-as-user qualitative reporting without claiming broad generalization — establishes the genre legitimacy the Rafiq paper relies on.

### Cluster D — Critical HCI and postphenomenology

#### 9. Suchman, L. (2007). *Human-machine reconfigurations: Plans and situated actions* (2nd ed.).
> **APA 7 entry:** Suchman, L. (2007). *Human-machine reconfigurations: Plans and situated actions* (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.

- **Type:** Foundational HCI/STS monograph (2nd ed.).
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Classic critique of plan-based HCI design; argues human–machine interaction is constituted in situ, not from pre-stored plans. The 2007 second edition reframes the original 1987 argument for contemporary hybrid human–machine assemblages. For Rafiq, supplies the philosophical defense of building in state-mutation + emergent dialogue over pre-scripted prompts — the psychological state machine is exactly the kind of "situated reconfiguration" Suchman advocates.

#### 10. Verbeek, P.-P. (2005). *What things do: Philosophical reflections on technology, agency, and design*.
> **APA 7 entry:** Verbeek, P.-P. (2005). *What things do: Philosophical reflections on technology, agency, and design* (R. P. Crease, Trans.). Penn State Press.

- **Type:** Philosophy-of-technology monograph.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Articulates postphenomenology's central claim that technologies mediate human perception and action rather than being neutral tools. Frames design as a moral activity because artifacts shape what users can do and want. Direct philosophical backing for the Rafiq paper's ethical-design section — the Soul Engine is a "mediating artifact" in Verbeek's sense, which is why its design choices carry ethical weight.

#### 11. Rosenberger, R. (2018). A postphenomenological method for HCI research.
> **APA 7 entry:** Rosenberger, R. (2018). A postphenomenological method for HCI research. In *Proceedings of the 30th Australian Conference on Computer-Human Interaction (OzCHI '18)* (pp. 242–251). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3292147.3292170

- **Type:** Conference methodology paper.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Translates Verbeek's postphenomenology into an HCI-research method: variational cross-examination of multiple "stabilities" of a single artifact. Useful operationally because it gives the Rafiq paper a method for analyzing how Rafiq mediates different users (isolated vs. socially embedded) differently — fitting the paper's "target population is plural" stance.

### Cluster E — Trustworthiness criteria for design case studies

#### 12. Venable, J., Pries-Heje, J., & Baskerville, R. (2012). FEDS: A framework for evaluation in design science research.
> **APA 7 entry:** Venable, J., Pries-Heje, J., & Baskerville, R. (2012). FEDS: A framework for evaluation in design science research. *European Journal of Information Systems*, *21*(1), 77–89. https://doi.org/10.1057/ejis.2011.36

- **Type:** Methodology journal article.
- **Tier:** 1.
- **Note (50–80 w):** Distinguishes artificial, natural, ex ante, and ex post evaluations and shows how a design researcher selects among them. For a single-developer case like Rafiq that lacks user studies, the framework legitimates "naturalistic + ex post + autoethnographic" as a defensible evaluation posture rather than a missing one. Surfaces the rigorous criteria (construct validity, internal consistency, replication logic, result generalizability) the Rafiq paper must address even when no controlled study exists.

## 3. Themes

**Theme 1 — Two methodological pillars are jointly required.** The DSR canon (Hevner et al. 2004; Peffers et al. 2007; Walls et al. 1992) supplies the artifact-evaluation grammar; the autoethnographic / autobiographical-design lineage (Chang 2008; Kaltenhauser et al. 2024; Neustaedter & Sengers 2012; Desjardins & Wakkary 2016; Voida & Mynatt 2009) supplies the reflexive voice a single-developer case needs. The Venable et al. (2012) framework is what makes the combination rigorous rather than ad hoc.

**Theme 2 — Postphenomenology reframes "design choices" as "mediations."** Suchman (2007) and Verbeek (2005) jointly argue that interactive artifacts are not neutral channels but constitutive of user perception and action. Rosenberger (2018) extends this into an HCI-operational method. For Rafiq, this means every Soul Engine / state-machine / dialect-pack choice is a moral choice, not a stylistic one — which is what the paper's ethical-design section claims.

**Theme 3 — The "I built this" voice is a method, not a confession.** The autobiographical-design tradition (Neustaedter & Sengers 2012; Desjardins & Wakkary 2016; Voida & Mynatt 2009) demonstrates that lived-with-an-artifact reflections are publishable, citable, and citable-as-evidence inside top venues (CHI, DIS). The autoethnography literature (Chang 2008; Kaltenhauser et al. 2024) provides the writing conventions that make the reflection analytically credible rather than confessional.
