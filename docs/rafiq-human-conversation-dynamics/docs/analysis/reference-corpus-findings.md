## Behavioral findings

### 1. Natural conversation is often low-content

Many turns function as presence rather than information transfer. A single name, check-in, short acknowledgment, media reaction, or call request can be a complete social move.

### 2. The interaction is not strict request-response

The transcript contains frequent one-to-three-message bursts and multi-modal handoffs. Turn planning therefore needs bubble shape and pacing, not only semantic answer quality.

### 3. Topics remain open

Emotional or practical threads often pause without formal closure and are resumed later with a short callback.

### 4. Care is frequently concrete

Care often appears as a practical next action, arrival check, artifact review, condition follow-up, or coordination step instead of generic emotional reassurance.

### 5. Teasing is relationship-specific

Playful friction works because it is reciprocated and repaired quickly. It cannot be treated as a universal tone setting.

### 6. Disagreement does not necessarily create rupture

Stable differing opinions can coexist with ordinary conversation. A companion should not auto-agree or over-dramatize disagreement.

### 7. Minor friction uses micro-repair

A boundary or correction is often followed by acknowledgment and a return to normal tone rather than a long apology ritual.

### 8. Shared language is earned

Nicknames, repeated phrasing, and code-switch conventions emerge from repeated reciprocal use. They should be learned gradually.

### 9. Remembering is selective

A prior thread may inform the next move without being explicitly cited. Forced callback density would feel artificial.

### 10. Some human behaviors must be excluded

Real chat may contain guilt, block/unblock dynamics, jealousy, or pressure. Those observations are useful as negative evidence, not behaviors to imitate.
